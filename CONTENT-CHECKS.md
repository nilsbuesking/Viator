## Historical and linguistic claims (Background and Pronunciation)
- [ ] Praeneste fibula: wording *Manios med fhefhaked Numasioi*, the dispute over authenticity, and the 2011 examinations.
- [ ] Lapis Niger and Duenos inscription "usually dated to the sixth century BC".
- [ ] Latin alphabet reaching Rome through the Etruscans from a western Greek alphabet.
- [ ] Kant's Latin dissertations at Königsberg; Gauss, *Disquisitiones arithmeticae*, 1801.
- [ ] Cognate table (pater/Vater, piscis/Fisch, trēs/drei, duo/zwei, cornū/Horn, centum/hundert, genū/Knie, est/ist, sunt/sind) and the summary of the first and second consonant shifts.
- [ ] Loanwords: Mauer, Fenster, Ziegel, Keller, Straße, Wein, Kiste, Pforte, Pfund, Kaiser; that Old High German *w* was still [w].
- [ ] Greek spellings Κικέρων and Οὐαλέριος.
- [ ] Great Vowel Shift dates (c. 1400–1700) and the parallel Middle High German diphthongisation (mîn → mein, hûs → Haus).
- [ ] Open short vowels: the Romance merger argument (short i with long ē; short u with long ō).
- [ ] Unaspirated p, t, c: the argument from the spellings ph, th, ch.
- [ ] Traditional German school pronunciation ("Zizero", "Zäsar") and the statement that practice varies.
- [ ] W. Sidney Allen, *Vox Latina*, 2nd edition 1978.

## Grammar section claims
- [ ] Shortening of long vowels before final -t, -nt, -m, -r.
- [ ] Ablative as the merger of ablative, instrumental and locative.
- [ ] Rhotacism dated to the fourth century BC; Cicero, *Ad familiares* IX.21 on Papisius/Papirius.
- [ ] German war/gewesen as a comparable s/r alternation (Verner's law, a different cause).
- [ ] Case order: German school grammars N G D A Abl (V); Kennedy's *Latin Primer* N V A G D Ab.
- [ ] Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 82, on Caesar's last words.
- [ ] Vocabulary note: German *irren* as a probable cognate of *errāre*; *Vieh* and *fee* as cognates of *pecus*.
