# Source texts (not loaded by the app)

Texts supplied by Nils for building readings. Public domain.

- `beowulf-klaeber-1922-lines-1-2601.txt`: Beowulf, Klaeber's edition (1922), as transcribed
  online without length marks; lines 1–2601 only; continued in `klaeber-beowulf-pages/` (page images). Known transcription
  slips: missing spaces (e.g. "syððanærest", "feohgiftumon", "billum ond byrnum;him").
- `beowulf-poetryfoundation-full.txt`: full Old English text (lines 1–3182) from the Poetry
  Foundation website. Not Klaeber: line 9 reads "æghwylc þara ymbsittendra" and punctuation
  differs; the source edition is not named (it appears to follow a mid-20th-century edition,
  possibly Dobbie's Anglo-Saxon Poetic Records, 1953; unconfirmed). File line numbers match
  the poem's line numbers. Planned use: Lecture XI (lines 3137–3182), labelled with this source.
- `beowulf-mit-bilingual-gummere.txt`: bilingual text from an MIT web page, OE text matching
  the Poetry Foundation version, with Francis B. Gummere's verse translation (1910). Converted
  from PDF: the ligatures fi/fl are lost ("ndan" = findan, "lirea" = Liffrea), so use only to
  cross-check, not as a source.
- `maldon-anglosaxonarchaeology.txt`: The Battle of Maldon, lines 1–325, from the website
  "Anglo-Saxon Archaeology" (edition not named). Line numbers every five lines label the line
  that follows. Capital Æ of names lost (æþelredes, ælfere).
- `chaucer-skeat-1894-canterbury-tales.txt`: The Canterbury Tales in Skeat's edition
  (The Complete Works of Geoffrey Chaucer, vol. 4, Oxford 1894), Project Gutenberg #22120.
  Arranged by Skeat's Groups A–I with his line numbers per group (General Prologue = A 1–858).
  Line numbers stand on their own line before every fifth line; each passage is followed by
  Skeat's variant-reading notes (lines beginning "Heading." or "1. E. …"), which must be
  stripped when extracting text. Sigla: E = Ellesmere, Hn = Hengwrt, Hl = Harley 7334, etc.
- `latin-readings.txt`: authentic Latin readings supplied by Nils for the later Latin lectures:
  Caesar BG 1.1, Catullus 5 and 85, Cicero Cat. 1.1–2, Virgil Aen. 1.1–11, Horace Odes 1.11,
  with notes on small slips in the copies. `latin-pliny-ep-6-16.txt`: Pliny, Letters 6.16 (complete). `latin-pliny-ep-6-20.txt`: Pliny, Letters 6.20 (complete; supplied by Nils; the Virgil quotation in §1 (Aen. 2.12–13) completed from Nils’s correction).
- `oe-sweet-reader-1879-ocr.txt`: Henry Sweet, *An Anglo-Saxon Reader in Prose and Verse*, 2nd edition
  (Oxford 1879; public domain), full text from the Internet Archive, supplied by Nils. Machine OCR of poor
  quality for Old English (ð read as S or 8, accents and ǫ lost): use only to find passages and page numbers;
  every reading must be transcribed from the page images.
- `oe-sweet-1879-cynewulf-cyneheard.txt`: Chronicle annal 755 (Cynewulf and Cyneheard, Parker MS.) from
  Sweet's Reader, 2nd ed., pp. 1–3, transcribed from page images supplied by Nils. Sweet's own marks kept
  (acute = long vowel, ǫ, ę); the course will convert to macrons and normalise ǫ/ę, saying so.
- `oe-sweet-1879-alfred-preface.txt`: King Alfred's Preface to the Pastoral Care ("On the State of Learning
  in England") from Sweet's Reader, 2nd ed., pp. 4–7, transcribed from page images supplied by Nils, with
  Sweet's footnotes (Hatton MS. text, Cotton variants). Sweet's marks kept as printed.
- `oe-sweet-1879-maldon.txt`: The Battle of Maldon, complete (lines 1–325), with Sweet's introduction and
  the Chronicle entry he quotes, from Sweet's Reader, 2nd ed., no. XIX, pp. 133–144, transcribed from page
  images supplied by Nils, with Sweet's footnotes (forms of the 18th-century transcript). Since November 2026
  the source of the Maldon special lecture, in place of `maldon-anglosaxonarchaeology.txt`.
- `klaeber-beowulf-pages/p098.png` … `p117.png`: page images of Klaeber's Beowulf, pp. 98–117 (lines 2598–3131,
  with Klaeber's apparatus), supplied by Nils. Edition (1922 or a later Klaeber edition) to be confirmed from the
  title page. Pages carry a former reader's handwritten notes, which are not part of the text. Lines 3132–3182
  (pp. 118–119) still needed. Passages are transcribed from these images when a lecture uses them.
