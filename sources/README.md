# Source texts (not loaded by the app)

Texts supplied by Nils for building readings. Public domain.

- `beowulf-klaeber-1922-lines-1-2601.txt`: Beowulf, Klaeber's edition (1922), as transcribed
  online without length marks; lines 1–2601 only; continued in `klaeber-beowulf-pages/` (page images). Known transcription
  slips: missing spaces (e.g. "syððanærest", "feohgiftumon", "billum ond byrnum;him").
- `beowulf-poetryfoundation-full.txt`: full Old English text (lines 1–3182) from the Poetry
  Foundation website. Not Klaeber: line 9 reads "æghwylc þara ymbsittendra" and punctuation
  differs; the source edition is not named (it appears to follow a mid-20th-century edition,
  possibly Dobbie's Anglo-Saxon Poetic Records, 1953; unconfirmed). File line numbers match
  the poem's line numbers. Planned use: Lecture XI (lines 3137–3182), labelled with this source.
- `beowulf-mit-bilingual-gummere.txt`: bilingual text from an MIT web page, OE text matching
  the Poetry Foundation version, with Francis B. Gummere's verse translation (1910). Converted
  from PDF: the ligatures fi/fl are lost ("ndan" = findan, "lirea" = Liffrea), so use only to
  cross-check, not as a source.
- `maldon-anglosaxonarchaeology.txt`: The Battle of Maldon, lines 1–325, from the website
  "Anglo-Saxon Archaeology" (edition not named). Line numbers every five lines label the line
  that follows. Capital Æ of names lost (æþelredes, ælfere).
- `chaucer-skeat-1894-canterbury-tales.txt`: The Canterbury Tales in Skeat's edition
  (The Complete Works of Geoffrey Chaucer, vol. 4, Oxford 1894), Project Gutenberg #22120.
  Arranged by Skeat's Groups A–I with his line numbers per group (General Prologue = A 1–858).
  Line numbers stand on their own line before every fifth line; each passage is followed by
  Skeat's variant-reading notes (lines beginning "Heading." or "1. E. …"), which must be
  stripped when extracting text. Sigla: E = Ellesmere, Hn = Hengwrt, Hl = Harley 7334, etc.
- `latin-readings.txt`: authentic Latin readings supplied by Nils for the later Latin lectures:
  Caesar BG 1.1, Catullus 5 and 85, Cicero Cat. 1.1–2, Virgil Aen. 1.1–11, Horace Odes 1.11,
  with notes on small slips in the copies. `latin-pliny-ep-6-16.txt`: Pliny, Letters 6.16 (complete). `latin-pliny-ep-6-20.txt`: Pliny, Letters 6.20 (complete; supplied by Nils; the Virgil quotation in §1 (Aen. 2.12–13) completed from Nils’s correction).
- `oe-sweet-reader-1879-ocr.txt`: Henry Sweet, *An Anglo-Saxon Reader in Prose and Verse*, 2nd edition
  (Oxford 1879; public domain), full text from the Internet Archive, supplied by Nils. Machine OCR of poor
  quality for Old English (ð read as S or 8, accents and ǫ lost): use only to find passages and page numbers;
  every reading must be transcribed from the page images.
- `oe-sweet-1879-cynewulf-cyneheard.txt`: Chronicle annal 755 (Cynewulf and Cyneheard, Parker MS.) from
  Sweet's Reader, 2nd ed., pp. 1–3, transcribed from page images supplied by Nils. Sweet's own marks kept
  (acute = long vowel, ǫ, ę); the course will convert to macrons and normalise ǫ/ę, saying so.
- `oe-sweet-1879-alfred-preface.txt`: King Alfred's Preface to the Pastoral Care ("On the State of Learning
  in England") from Sweet's Reader, 2nd ed., pp. 4–7, transcribed from page images supplied by Nils, with
  Sweet's footnotes (Hatton MS. text, Cotton variants). Sweet's marks kept as printed.
- `oe-sweet-1879-maldon.txt`: The Battle of Maldon, complete (lines 1–325), with Sweet's introduction and
  the Chronicle entry he quotes, from Sweet's Reader, 2nd ed., no. XIX, pp. 133–144, transcribed from page
  images supplied by Nils, with Sweet's footnotes (forms of the 18th-century transcript). Since November 2026
  the source of the Maldon special lecture, in place of `maldon-anglosaxonarchaeology.txt`.
- `klaeber-beowulf-pages/p098.png` … `p120.png` and `title.png`: page images of Fr. Klaeber, *Beowulf and the Fight
  at Finnsburg* (Boston: D. C. Heath), pp. 98–120 (lines 2598–3182, end of the poem, with Klaeber's apparatus),
  supplied by Nils. The title page carries no date, but the library accession stamp of 22.1.23 places the copy
  before the second edition (1928), so it is the first edition of 1922. Pages carry a former reader's handwritten
  notes and an ink blot (pp. 117–118), which are not part of the text. On pp. 118–119 (lines 3150–3182) the many
  round brackets are Klaeber's: the last page of the manuscript is damaged and partly freshened up by a later
  hand, and letters in ( ) are his restorations, [ ] his additions. Passages are transcribed from these images
  when a lecture uses them.
- `nds-schaumburg-texts.txt`: texts in Schaumburg Platt supplied by Nils (2026-11-07): a prose piece by Karl Wehmeyer
  on the evening twilight (*Schummernisse*), an anonymous anecdote (“Reget et noch?”), and twelve proverbs with German
  renderings. Source: website of the Arbeitsgruppe Spurensuche in der Schaumburger Landschaft. Wehmeyer’s text and the anecdote are probably in copyright:
  quote only short phrases in the app; the proverbs are traditional.
- Further Skeat volumes supplied by Nils (2026-11-11), all from Project Gutenberg, same edition and layout as the
  Canterbury Tales file (line numbers on their own line before every fifth line; Skeat's variant notes follow each
  page and must be stripped when extracting text):
  - `chaucer-skeat-1894-vol1-romaunt-minor-poems.txt`: vol. 1 (#43089): Life of Chaucer, Romaunt of the Rose,
    Minor Poems (A. B. C., Book of the Duchesse, Parlement of Foules, Truth, Purse, etc.).
  - `chaucer-skeat-1894-vol2-boece-troilus.txt`: vol. 2 (#44833): Boece and Troilus and Criseyde (Books I–V),
    with Skeat's introduction on Boccaccio's *Filostrato* and Guido delle Colonne.
  - `chaucer-skeat-1894-vol3-fame-legend-astrolabe.txt`: vol. 3 (#45027): House of Fame, Legend of Good Women (both
    versions of the Prologue), Astrolabe, and the Sources of the Canterbury Tales. Quotes Gower's first-recension
    greeting to Chaucer twice (introduction to the Legend, p. xliii; Sources, p. 414) from Pauli's edition (1857,
    iii. 374), with spelling corrected by Skeat. Skeat dates the first recension 1385; Macaulay (vol. 2 below) 1390.
- Gower, supplied by Nils (2026-11-11):
  - `gower-macaulay-1901-vol2-confessio-prol-v.txt`: G. C. Macaulay, *The Complete Works of John Gower*, vol. 2,
    The English Works (Oxford 1901): *Confessio Amantis*, Prologue to Book V l. 1970, with Macaulay's introduction
    (date and recensions, language, manuscripts) and notes. Project Gutenberg #71162 (proofread). Vol. 3 (Books
    VI–VIII, with the first-recension epilogue) is Gutenberg #71433, not yet supplied.
  - `gower-confessio-amantis-gutenberg266.txt`: *Confessio Amantis*, all eight books, “based on” Macaulay's text,
    Project Gutenberg #266 (2008). No introduction, notes or variants; the first-recension lines (e.g. the
    greeting to Chaucer) are absent. Use only where vol. 2 does not reach, and check against Macaulay.
  - `gower-macaulay-1902-vol4-latin-ocr.txt`: Macaulay vol. 4, The Latin Works (1902), Internet Archive OCR:
    Life of Gower, *Vox Clamantis* (Book I on the rising of 1381), *Cronica Tripertita*. OCR quality fair; Nils
    has the PDF (completeworksofj04goweuoft.pdf).
- `langland-skeat-piers-plowman-b-ocr.txt`: W. W. Skeat, *The Vision of William concerning Piers the Plowman*
  (B-text, “about A.D. 1377”), Prologue and Passus I–VII, tenth edition revised (Oxford, Clarendon Press; the library
  shelfmark of this copy carries the date 1923), Internet Archive OCR of a Google scan (University of Michigan). OCR only: Nils has the PDF of the
  page images (Skeat_The_Vision_Concerning_10th_ed.pdf, 272 pages, 75 MB, not kept in the repository); readings
  must be transcribed from the pages.

## Middle High German (supplied by Nils, November 2026; texts only, the scans are not kept in the repo)

All public domain. Machine OCR or typed text: use these to find passages and page numbers; every reading must be
transcribed from page images, and every form checked against the printed edition.

- `mhd-nibelungenlied-bartsch-1866-ocr.txt`: Bartsch, *Das Nibelungenlied* (Deutsche Classiker des Mittelalters 3,
  ed. Pfeiffer, Leipzig 1866), archive.org OCR. Poor for diacritics (circumflexes read as ä/ö, æ lost). The scan
  is clean; it prints Lachmann's stanza numbers in brackets. Text follows the *B* reworking (his introduction);
  the exact manuscript basis is unchecked.
- `mhd-hartmann-iwein-bech-1869-ocr.txt`: Bech, *Hartmann von Aue*, vol. 3 (*Iwein*), Leipzig: Brockhaus 1869
  (Google scan, archive.org OCR). Base manuscript: Heidelberg (Lachmann's method). OCR reads ê as ä, æ as "£".
  Line numbering still to be checked against Benecke–Lachmann before citing Iw. verse numbers.
- `mhd-hartmann-armer-heinrich-paul-1921-ocr.txt`: Paul, *Der arme Heinrich*, Altdeutsche Textbibliothek 3, 6th ed.,
  Halle 1921. Short and complete; OCR of the scan.
- `mhd-wolfram-parzival-lachmann-1891-typed.txt`: Lachmann's *Parzival*, 5th ed., Berlin 1891, extracted from a
  typed (not scanned) PDF of unknown origin. Section and line numbers follow Lachmann (114,8 = "ich bin Wolfram
  von Eschenbach") but the number column has slips (e.g. 113,25 and 113,30 stacked): check numbering and wording
  against a scan before quoting.
- `mhd-berthold-pfeiffer-vol1-textlayer.txt`: Berthold von Regensburg, *Vollständige Ausgabe seiner Predigten*,
  vol. 1, ed. Pfeiffer (Vienna: Braumüller; year not confirmed from the title page); text layer of the Google scan.
  Page 21 of the scan is clean, with a line count every five lines.
- `mhd-berthold-strobl-vol2-1880-ocr.txt`: the same, vol. 2 (Predigten XXXVII–LXXI, introduction and notes by
  Strobl), Vienna 1880. Poor OCR; no scan available to us.
- `mhd-schwabenspiegel-lassberg-1840-frontmatter-ocr.txt`: Lassberg, *Der Schwabenspiegel … nach einer Handschrift
  vom Jahr 1287*, Tübingen 1840, preface by Reyscher: **only the front matter** (manuscript descriptions, c. 95
  pages); the law text itself is not in the file.
- `mhd-urkundenbuch-mittelrhein-vol3-1874-ocr.txt`: Eltester and Goerz, *Urkundenbuch zur Geschichte der
  mittelrheinischen Territorien*, vol. 3 (1212–1260), Coblenz 1874. Almost entirely Latin. The German text no. 260
  ("1225", Simon von Schöneck's will) is a **much later German translation**, not a 13th-century original.
- `mhd-lexer-taschenwoerterbuch-ocr.txt`: Lexer, *Mittelhochdeutsches Taschenwörterbuch*, 3rd ed. (year not
  confirmed). Searchable; occasional OCR slips.
- `mhd-weinhold-grammatik-1877-ocr.txt`: Weinhold, *Mittelhochdeutsche Grammatik*, Paderborn: Schöningh 1877 (first
  edition; the later editions differ). Scan pages are clean and numbered by §.
- `mhd-simrock-parzival-titurel-translation-ocr.txt`: Simrock's modern German verse translation of Parzival and
  Titurel (Stuttgart), Fraktur OCR of poor quality. A translation, not a source for the Middle High German text.
- `mhd-weston-parzival-vol1-english.txt`, `mhd-weston-parzival-vol2-english.txt`: Weston's English translation of
  Parzival (London 1894 reprint), Project Gutenberg #47297 and #47298. Background only.
- Not in the repo (no text file): Lachmann, *Die Gedichte Walthers von der Vogelweide*, 2nd ed., Berlin 1843
  (image-only Google scan; page and line numbers confirmed, "Under der linden" = 39,11).
